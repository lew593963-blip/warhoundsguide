Choose a Warhounds weapon for the job it must perform, then build the position that lets it perform that job. The official material confirms a broad arsenal, upgradable equipment, drones, combat scanners, smoke, optical camouflage, visible lines of fire, high ground, destructible cover, vertical play, true ballistics, and cone-based overwatch. It does not publish a complete evidence-safe damage table or universal best-weapon tier list.

Use five loadout questions:

1. What distance and angle must this soldier control?
2. Does the squad need information, movement protection, pressure, or cover removal?
3. Which terrain can help—and which terrain can disappear?
4. Who acts after this weapon or tool creates an opening?
5. What is the fallback when the first position fails?

That framework is more reliable than choosing equipment by one displayed stat or an unsupported ranking.

## What is officially confirmed

The [Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3929470/Warhounds/?l=english) describes weapons and equipment you can upgrade, plus drones, combat scanners, smoke, and optical camouflage. It emphasizes visible firing lines, high ground, true ballistics, destructible cover, vertical gameplay, and cone-based overwatch.

The [official site](https://warhounds.game/) also describes a wide selection of weapons, gear, abilities, and fully destructible environments.

Those are strong system-level facts. The sources checked here do not establish every gun name, damage value, range breakpoint, armor interaction, or best-in-slot choice. This guide therefore works at the tactical-job level.

## Choose weapons by tactical job

| Tactical job | What the loadout must accomplish | Positioning requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Safe observation | reveal danger without committing the whole squad | protected sight or a remote information route |
| Lane control | make an approach costly or predictable | visibility, stable cone, and a second response |
| Distant threat | pressure a target across an open or vertical space | useful line, high ground when safe, planned escape |
| Cover disruption | remove protection or force movement | spacing, follow-up fire, and awareness of friendly exposure |
| Protected crossing | reduce the cost of moving through danger | smoke, concealment, alternate angle, or overwatch support |
| Objective push | convert a temporary opening into progress | coordinated movement and a fallback tile |

One weapon may cover more than one job, and one class name does not prove ownership of every tool. Match the current item description and soldier capability to the problem visible in your build.

## Drones and combat scanners: buy information

Drones and scanners are named official tools. Their most important value is not abstract discovery; it is preventing the squad from spending movement and exposure on a guess.

Use an information tool before:

- entering an unknown lane;
- committing a close or forward role;
- leaving reliable cover;
- building an overwatch cone that depends on an unseen approach;
- moving a long-range unit to high ground;
- grouping soldiers around terrain that may be vulnerable.

Information becomes wasted action when nobody is ready to use it. Plan the next move before scanning: who attacks, moves, watches, or withdraws when the threat appears?

## Smoke and optical camouflage: protect a decision

Smoke and optical camouflage are also officially named. Use them to make a necessary action safer, not merely because they are available.

A useful concealment action should enable something specific:

- cross a dangerous lane;
- rescue a bad position;
- reach the objective;
- create a new firing angle;
- break contact;
- protect the setup for another tool.

The exact visibility and duration rules should be read from the current build rather than assumed. The evidence-safe strategic principle is that concealment changes exposure; it does not remove the need for spacing, support, and an exit plan.

## True ballistics: build the shot before taking it

Steam markets Warhounds around true ballistics and contrasts that design with “fake RNG.” That official wording does not expose every internal formula. It does support a combat style centered on geometry and physical firing lines.

Before firing, improve the shot by checking:

- line of fire;
- intervening cover;
- elevation;
- the target's ability to move or return fire;
- whether another unit can create a better angle first;
- what happens to your shooter after the attack.

Do not reduce “true ballistics” to “every visible shot must hit.” Use the interface and current encounter to evaluate the physical line, and avoid claiming an unpublished calculation.

## Destructible cover changes every firing lane

Cover can be both protection and a temporary resource. A position that is strong now may become exposed after sustained or explosive pressure.

Use destruction in two directions:

- **Offensively:** remove enemy protection, open a new line, or force relocation.
- **Defensively:** anticipate which friendly protection the enemy can remove and keep a second position reachable.

When you destroy cover, immediately ask who benefits from the new line. Opening terrain without controlling it can expose your own advance.

Spacing is essential. If several soldiers depend on one piece of terrain, one change can break the whole formation.

## High ground and vertical play

High ground is officially emphasized, but elevation is valuable only when the squad can reach, support, and leave it.

Before committing upward, check:

- whether the route is exposed;
- which targets become visible;
- which new enemies can see the position;
- whether destructible terrain undermines it;
- whether another soldier can support the angle;
- whether the objective will soon move elsewhere.

A lower angle that supports the objective may be better than an isolated elevated firing point. Height is a tool, not the mission.

## Cone-based overwatch

Cone overwatch turns facing and lane choice into resource decisions. The best cone is not necessarily the widest or the one aimed at the nearest enemy. It is the one covering the route the enemy must or is most likely to use.

Build overwatch in four steps:

1. identify the movement lane that matters;
2. make sure the watching unit can actually see it;
3. keep allies from blocking or depending on the same fragile cover;
4. retain a second response if the enemy uses another route.

Overwatch also supports movement. One unit shapes the danger lane while another crosses, flanks, or advances the objective.

## Combine the squad, tool, and terrain layers

A coherent combat plan links three things:

- **role:** which mercenary owns the tactical job;
- **tool:** which weapon, scanner, drone, smoke, or concealment option enables it;
- **terrain:** which line, cover, elevation, or route makes it work.

If one layer is missing, change the plan before committing. A [balanced squad](/squad-guide) without the right tool cannot solve the job. The right tool in an unusable position is equally weak. A strong position without a soldier able to exploit it is just scenery.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best weapon in Warhounds?

The checked official sources do not establish one universal best weapon. Choose for the tactical job, firing line, terrain, squad role, and mission objective.

### Does Warhounds have true ballistics?

Yes. The Steam listing explicitly describes true ballistics and contrasts the combat with fake random-number outcomes. It does not publish every internal calculation.

### Is cover destructible?

Yes. Both official sources describe destructible environments or cover. Always plan a second position.

### What tactical tools are confirmed?

Drones, combat scanners, smoke, and optical camouflage are named on the current Steam listing.

### How does overwatch work?

Steam describes cone-based overwatch. Aim the cone at a meaningful movement lane, support it with visibility and a fallback response, and verify exact current-build behavior in the game interface.
